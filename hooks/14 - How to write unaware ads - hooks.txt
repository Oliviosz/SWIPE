--- BEGIN ORIGINAL WRITTEN LESSON ---

Unaware ads appeal to the largest market, but are the hardest to create. 

Which is why when you crack them, they can become your most scalable ads that convert the largest % of the market.

[IMAGE 01: Images/14-image-01.png. See the illustrated reading copy; image text is not transcribed here.]

As indicated by the diagram above, nearly 60%+ (depending who you considered can be sold) are sitting in the unaware stage.

Unaware ads target people who are not actively seeking a solution because they either:

  1. Don't recognize they have a problem 

  2. Don't realize a new way to satisfy their specific desire

These are the people, to quote Eugene: 

"who are logical prospects for your product and yet, in their own minds, are hundreds of miles away from accepting that product."

By identifying with our prospect in the hook, they will feel as they are watching or reading a piece of our content and through that content become aware of the problem that exists, or become aware of a way to satisfy their desire that they were not aware of.

----------------------------------------

Characteristics of an Unaware Ad

1. It's Camouflaged

It doesn't look, feel, or sound like an ad. It looks like:

  - Educational/entertaining piece of content

  - Story someone is sharing

  - Information they genuinely want to consume

But: Camouflage ≠ Unaware. 

You can camouflage a problem-aware ad too. The difference is what you're camouflaging around.

2. Identity Over Product

The hook must be their headline, their problem, their state of mind, even if they don't know it's a problem yet. You're giving words to something they feel but haven't articulated.

When it comes to unaware ads, it is important to understand:

  1. Price means nothing to them. 

  2. The name of your product means nothing to them. 

  3. What your product does, what benefit it provides, or the problem it solves all means nothing to them. 

With starting with unaware ads, you have to forget about your product and concentrate on your prospect's identity.

Your hook & your headlines purpose is to sell the remainder of the ad itself. 

3. The Sequence (at least how it should be)

  1. Unaware → Grab their attention with an identity driven hook/headline

  2. Problem Aware → Make them aware of a desire or a problem/future problem they will have

  3. Solution Aware → Show them what types of solution exists

  4. Product Aware → Position your product as the one that solves it the best for them

  5. Most Aware → Make them an offer that makes an impulse buying decision, easy.

----------------------------------------

Eugene's Examples For Unaware Ads:

  1. Giving words to a hidden dream - The University of the night 

  2. Exploiting a hidden fear - Why men crack... 

  3. Leading into an unacceptable problem by starting with a universally accepted image - Within the curve of a woman's arm 

  4. Using a common objection to capture a greater market - Why haven't TV owners been told these facts? 

  5. Projecting an ultimate triumph that prospects will identify with - They laughed when I sat down at the piano, but when I started to play!⎯

  6. Projecting the result of a problem that avoids rejection that a direct statement would cause - Often a bridesmaid, but never a bride 

  7. Projecting the result of an accomplishment to attract people who don't want to put in the effort - Here's an extra $50, Grace. 

----------------------------------------

Evolve Simplified Strategies For Unaware Ads:

The Openers:

1. Hidden Fear 

Tap into fears everyone shares but don't talk about daily.

Hook structure: Lead with the fear, tell a story around it, then bridge to your solution.

2. Hidden Desire 

Tap into desires everyone shares but don't talk about daily.

Hook structure: Lead with the desire, tell a story around it, then bridge to your solution.

3. Did You Know

Taps into the minds genuine curiosity. 

Hook structure: Lead with a question, stat, or story that can be bridged to the problem

4. Debate

Taps into the minds love for taking sides. Standing for something

Hook structure: Lead with an open-ended question/comparison, bridge to the problem.
