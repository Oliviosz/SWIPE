--- BEGIN ORIGINAL WRITTEN LESSON ---

Once your hook has 1 goal, grabbed their attention.

Your bridge also has 1 goal, keep their attention

The goal of a great bridge is to MAKE SURE we keep audience hooked in & engaged. 

The bridge is the most important part to decide whether or not someone keeps watching or they drop off. 

Bridges can also be sometimes great hooks. 

This strategy is called in media res (more down below)

And a lot of times just by cutting the hook out and starting straight in the action can lead to breakthrough ads.

One of the $1,000,000 ads we made (single ad spent $1M) was actually just an in-media res hook (aka a bridge hook) where we cut off the original hook and started at the bridge. 

Sometimes you don't need to set up the bridge, and the bridge itself can be the hook. 

----------------------------------------

6 Ways To Write Effective Bridges:

#1 - Introduce the mechanism (Bridge back to benefit)

#2 - Escalate the problem (relatability/emotion) 

#3 - Escalate the desire (relatability/emotion) 

#4 - Reposition the problem (Ideally with an authority)

#5 - Allude to new information (ideally from authority)

#6 - Setting the timeline (how long/what you will explain)

Example Bridges:

#1 - Introduce the mechanism + tie back to promise (P-B-P)

Hook: if you have X, I need to show you this. 

Bridge: The X MECHANISM helped me get DESIRE (CONTROL). And the Y MECHANISM helps with COMMON PROBLEM.

#1 - Introduce the mechanism + tie back to promise (P-B-P)

Hook: Watch how this [PRODUCT] stops [PROBLEM]

Bridge: First, this [PRODUCT] has a [NEW MECHANISM]. And the [NEW MECHANISM] is what actually stops [PROBLEM] and causes [VISUAL SOLUTION OF PROBLEM]
